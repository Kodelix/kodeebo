## 1. Question

Two decisions the founder asked for after the staff-auth threat model (2026-10-19), one per part:

1. **M21 — approve-on-phone counter enrolment (part 1).** Today a person with a dashboard login enrols their counter PIN on the password path: they type username, password and a TOTP or recovery code on the counter (ADR-0007 § 7). Venue-PC malware can capture that (T3.12), and residual R15 is accepted until M21 lands.
   - The shape asked for: the counter shows a one-time code; the person approves from their own dashboard session on their phone, with step-up; no password is typed on the counter; the counter receives the existing single-use enrolment assertion, bound to its device credential.
   - To settle: what RFC 8628 and later guidance require; options; binding and consent; whether it can replace the password path for privileged people; the standards rows.
2. **M22 — account-takeover protections (part 2).** The shape is recorded, not re-opened:
   - an owner notice after N failed passwords;
   - a "second factor pending" notice with a "not me" action behind a confirming POST;
   - a breached-password check at set, with no password or full hash leaving the server;
   - server-protecting bot defence: throttle before any hash, a cap on concurrent Argon2 verifications, no image or puzzle CAPTCHA, never an IP ban alone.

   Open, and researched here: (1) the notice threshold N; (2) self-hosted proof-of-work versus no challenge, with third-party invisible checks as a compared option and their PDPL transfer cost; (3) the breached-password source; (4) throttle state across restarts.

The founder decides. `cto` records the ADR amendments, and `security-lead` updates the threat model.

Why the main alternatives lose:
- **A (OAuth server):** a second identity system, which ADR-0007 Alternative 2 rejected; BCP 247 also advises against the pattern for high-value resources (F121).
- **D (CIBA-style push):** anyone who can name the person can push requests at them (F115).
- **E (keep the password path):** leaves R15 open.
- **2C (Turnstile):** sends each client's IP address, TLS fingerprint and User-Agent to a vendor that states no processing region (F215).
- **3B (offline corpus):** about 81 GB, far above the download cap (F206).
- **4A and 4B:** lose durable blocks; 4B can't rebuild per-address state because events hold no full IP (F221).
- **4D (UNLOGGED):** the table is truncated after a Postgres crash (F218).
- **4E (Redis or Valkey):** adds a service, and its persistence is off by default (F216).

## 5. Decision

**Decided by the founder (Kamel Mohamed), 2026-10-20: every decision as the researchers recommended**, with the additions below. `cto` records the ADR amendments. The researchers' full reasoning is in the appendices: Part 1 § 5 and Part 2 § 5.

**Founder decisions, 2026-10-20:**
- **M21 flow: B.** The counter shows the code, and the person approves on their phone (conditions 1–12 of Part 1 § 5). Reverse direction (C) stays the documented fallback if `security-lead` finds the remote-approver case unacceptable.
- **Q-intended: name first.** An existing staff member picks their own name on the counter first. The consent screen says which counter claims to be them, and an approval by anyone else's session is refused and reported to the owner. The first owner at the guided install skips the pick.
- **Q-fallback: no counter enrolment without a second device.** Once B ships, the password path is refused for the owner and for anyone holding a privileged key. A privileged person with a dashboard login but no second device cannot enrol on a counter.
- **Q-matching: on for privileged people.** Number matching (B3) and a post-PIN "that was me" confirmation are on by default, and owner-visible, for anyone holding a privileged key. They are off for non-privileged people.
- **M22.1: 1C.** N = 10 consecutive wrong passwords on a known login, at most one notice per 24 h, with no action link. Another notice goes out when a correct password follows failures. Both numbers are configuration and no source gives them.
- **M22.2: 2A now.** No challenge. A self-hosted proof-of-work (ALTCHA + `altcha-lib-java`, MIT) is designed and built only if staff-auth-note S13 or M43 shows the Argon2 cap fails, or the credential-stuffing alert fires.
- **M22.3: 3A + 3C, failing open.** If HIBP is unreachable, the password is accepted when the bundled list passes. A `breach_check_skipped` event is written, and the password is rechecked at the next successful sign-in.
- **M22.4: narrow 4C.** A logged PostgreSQL table holds the per-known-login failure counts, the ceiling block and the "not me" block. Per-address delays stay in memory.
- **NOT SOURCEABLE items:** the founder confirmed none blocks these decisions; they are peripheral gaps with owners in § 9.

**A counter-only person who holds a privileged key** (founder, 2026-10-20) has no dashboard login: the owner sponsored them on a counter, and their roles carry a privileged key, such as an approver. This follows from Accepted rules, ADR-0008 § 10 item 3 and the 2026-10-19 amendment M11:
- **No route confirms their PIN today:**
  - no password path: they have no dashboard login, and the path is refused for privileged people anyway;
  - no approve-on-phone: it needs a dashboard session on a second device;
  - no owner confirmation: M11 forbids it for privileged people;
  - no activation code: it is for non-privileged people only.
- **So their sponsored PIN stays pending and valid only on the counter that minted it.** It is never delivered to another counter.
- **In a multi-counter venue they enrol on each counter separately,** by a new sponsored enrolment there, and each PIN works only on its own counter. They may set the same PIN on each; nothing compares them, since the server never sees a PIN. **Whether those per-counter pending PINs can coexist is open: Q-counteronly (§ 9).**
- **To confirm it later, they get a dashboard login and a second device.** The owner gives them a login; they enrol a second factor; they then enrol on any counter by approve-on-phone (B). That produces a confirmed verifier, which replaces every pending one and goes to every counter of their branches.
- **No other confirmation route is added.** An owner confirmation for privileged people would reopen the planted-PIN path that M11 closed (threat model T4.2, AB10).
- The owner dashboard should list a privileged counter-only person as "PIN per counter, unconfirmed", so the owner understands why an approver works only where they were sponsored.

The recommended options below are now the decisions.

1. **M21:** option **B**, an adapted RFC 8628 flow inside the Ktor backend with no OAuth server. Main conditions (the full list of 12 is in Part 1 § 5):
   - **Codes:** the user code is at least 40 bits, which means 10 base-20 characters, not the RFC's 8 (F125, F117). The request lasts at most 10 minutes and is single use, and a new request does not reset the failure count.
   - **Binding:** the request is bound to the device credential, tenant and branch.
   - **Step-up:** a fresh second factor bound to the request (new action `counter.enrol.approve`).
   - **Transfer:** the code must be transferred, typed or scanned; a compare-and-tap approval is refused, because NIST has withdrawn that method (F125).
   - **Consent screen:** it names the counter, the branch and the consequence.
   - **On the counter:** it shows the approver's name before the PIN screen.
   - **Notices:** the owner and the person are notified of every enrolment by this route.
   - **Frame protection** on the approval and login pages (F126).
   - **Password path:** refused for privileged people once B ships.
2. **M22.1:** **1C.** A notice after N = 10 consecutive wrong passwords within 24 h, at most one per window. A notice when the correct password follows failures. Notice (a) carries no action link.
3. **M22.2:** **2A now.** Refuse before any hash, a bounded Argon2 queue, and a reserved permit lane for known-device attempts. 2B (ALTCHA + `altcha-lib-java`, MIT) is designed and triggered only by staff-auth-note S13/M43 results or the credential-stuffing alert.
4. **M22.3:** **3A + 3C.** The HIBP range API from the backend only: the 5-hex prefix with `Add-Padding: true`. Plus a bundled top-100k or top-1M list as the always-on floor. Fail-open or fail-closed is your call.
5. **M22.4:** **narrow 4C.** A logged PostgreSQL table for the per-known-login failure counts, the ceiling block and the "not me" block, keyed by the throttle HMAC and expiring. Per-address delays stay in memory.

- **ADR-0007:** § 7 (third enrolment path), § 4 (new step-up action), § 3 (notices, throttle persistence) and § 2 (breached-password source).
- **ADR-0008:** § 10 item 3 (new confirmed fact kind).
- **Threat model:** M21, M22 and R15.

**Proposed pin changes:** none.

## 7. Performance & Caching Notes

The full notes are in Part 1 § 7 and Part 2 § 7; this summarises them.
- **Enrolment** is rare and off the selling path. The 3 s counter ceiling is read as excluding the human approval wait; you confirm that reading (Q-ceiling). Approval to PIN screen should meet the proposed 5 s p95 for a dashboard change reaching a counter (PENDING S13 in part 1).
- **One Argon2id verify at the floor** costs 13.3 ms and about 19 MiB on the test host (F219). The VPS figure is PENDING (staff-auth-note S13), and no sign-in or Argon2 budget exists in `perf-budgets.md` yet, so the cost is "not checked", never "met".
- **Cache decisions (gate 5):**
  - **Enrolment requests and codes:** no cache. They are secrets, stored in PostgreSQL with `expires_at`.
  - **Throttle state:** in process, plus a durable per-login row. Keys are throttle HMACs; a success resets the count; a reset clears the block and the known-device cookie hash.
  - **HIBP replies:** an in-process LRU by prefix, TTL 24 h, single-flight per prefix.
- **Pending:** the write cost of the throttle table (PENDING S24).

### Part 1 — 5. Decision

*Final (unit 19 of 20).* **Recommended - pending user decision.** The founder decides; `cto` records the ADR amendment.

**Chosen (recommended): B, the adapted device-authorization flow with no OAuth server, with these conditions** (each tied to a finding; none is a cited standard unless it says so):

1. **Keep RFC 8628's roles but not its server.** `device_code` ≥ 128 bits, never displayed, stored as a keyed hash; `user_code` of **at least 40 bits — 10 characters of the base-20 set (43.2 bits)**, not the RFC's 8 (34.6 bits), because NIST SP 800-63B-4 sets 40 bits as the floor for a binding code typed together with an identifier and 112 bits otherwise (F125, F117; applied by analogy); a QR handle, if any, ≥ 112 bits; stored as an HMAC with a server key (repo baseline, F120a); the request lives at most 10 minutes (ASVS 6.5.5, NIST 3.1.3.2; shorter by configuration; not the RFC example's 30 min or GitHub's 15 min, F121, F123), is single use, and a new request from the same device does not reset the failure counters (NIST, F125).
2. **Bind the request to the device, the tenant and the branch at creation**, from the device credential and not from anything the counter sends. Refuse the approval unless the approver's session is in the same tenant, the approver is assigned to that branch (or holds branches = All), and the device is active and lease-valid. This is the RFC 10027 § 6.1.7 "trusted device" mitigation and removes the case of a stranger's own counter phishing a victim from another tenant (design proposal).
3. **The approval needs a fresh second factor bound to the request** (ADR-0007 § 4 step-up with a new action `counter.enrol.approve` and the request id as target; a recovery code never satisfies it). A passkey-only person can therefore enrol, which the password path cannot offer (F120c).
4. **The consent screen states what approval does** (F107, F114, F125): the counter's label and branch, the time it asked, the user code, the person who will be enrolled (the approver's own name), and the consequence in one sentence — whoever is at that counter can then set a PIN in the approver's name that works on every counter of the branches they are assigned to (ADR-0008 § 10 item 1). Decline is as prominent as Approve. **The code must have been typed or scanned on the phone, not merely compared and tapped:** a compare-and-approve screen is the out-of-band method NIST has withdrawn (F125, F130), so an approval with no prior transfer of the code is refused (design rule derived from F125).
5. **The counter shows the approver's name after approval and before the PIN screen** and offers "Not me / Cancel", so a person who lost the race (RFC 8628 § 5.5, § 5.1) sees it. **The owner and the enrolled person are notified of every approve-on-phone enrolment** with a "not me" action (ASVS 6.3.5, 6.3.7; ties to M22).
6. **Optional hardening, owner-visible and default on for privileged persons (design proposal, founder to choose):** B3 number matching, and a second "that was me" confirmation on the phone after the PIN is set, before the verifier is delivered beyond the minting device.
7. **QR (B2) only as a convenience on top of B,** carrying a ≥ 128-bit handle, never replacing the typed code and the match step; not in the first release if the Play-wrap deep-link behaviour is unproven (NOT SOURCEABLE).
8. **Replace, then retire, the password path for privileged people.** Once B ships, the password path is refused for any person holding a privileged key and for the owner; it remains (owner-switchable) only for non-privileged dashboard users. A privileged person with no second device cannot enrol on a counter (decision for the founder: Q-fallback). This closes T3.12 / R15 **only** for approvals made on a second device (Q-samedevice).
9. **Send the approval to the counter by push or SSE nudge** where the sync design offers one (RFC 8628 § 3.5, F121a); otherwise the server-set `interval` ≤ 2 s (the RFC default of 5 s is a default, F102), because the human wait is not a counter action but the poll gap is added latency (§ 7).
10. **Optionally name the person at the counter first** (design proposal, answers the M21 deferred test "device code approved by another person's session is refused"): an existing staff member picks their own name from the branch list the device already holds; the request carries that person id; the consent screen then says "Counter 2 says it is you"; an approval by any other person's session is refused and reported to the owner. The first owner at the guided install has no list and enrols without the pick. Without this step the approver's session alone decides who is enrolled, and the M21 test has nothing to refuse (Q-intended).
11. **Frame protection on the approval and login pages** (CSP framing control; RFC 9700 names the device authorization endpoint and login pages, F126), so another page cannot overlay the Approve button.
12. **Proposed pin changes: none.** Nothing new is pinned; the optional QR renderer is unpinned (F127).

**Why not the others.** A: a second identity system the founder rejected (ADR-0007 Alt 2) and BCP 247 advises against for high-value resources (F121). D: anyone who can name the owner can push at them (F115, F116). C: the strongest first-line answer to unsolicited requests (the person must start), kept as the fallback if `security-lead` finds the remote-approver case in B unacceptable; its weakness is that the phone cannot show who is at the counter and the code-reading pretext is the same attack (F110 B8). E: leaves R15 open. F: not available on the counter. **ADR needed: yes** — an amendment to ADR-0007 § 7 (third enrolment path), ADR-0007 § 4 (new step-up action), ADR-0008 § 10 item 3 (a new confirmed fact kind) and the threat model (M21 text, R15).

*Final (unit 19 of 20).*

- **Abuse cases (≥ 1 required; each names the control):** (1) *consent phishing* — malware on counter 2 starts a request and a caller pressures the owner to approve; the owner is shown the counter, branch and the sentence of consequence, must match the code, and every approval notifies every owner contact; residual (F110). (2) *Cross-tenant lure* — a stranger who owns a legitimate counter of another tenant sends the owner a code; refused by the tenant check (condition 2). (3) *Session spying* — a customer sees the code and a co-worker with a dashboard login approves first; the counter shows the approver's name before the PIN screen (RFC 8628 § 5.5). (4) *Brute force of the code* — blind guessing of a 10-character base-20 code P ≈ 2.0e-11 at 10 attempts against 20 live codes (8 characters: 7.8e-9; F117), and a guessed code only enrols the guesser's own identity (RFC 8628 § 5.1); rate limits per approver session and per device. (5) *Approval bombing* — malware requests every few seconds; per-device request cap, one live request per device, the failure counter not reset by a new code (NIST 3.1.3.2). (6) *Same-device approval* — the owner opens the dashboard in a browser on the counter PC itself, which puts the password and TOTP back on the venue PC (R15 reopened); detection is a design proposal (Q-samedevice). (7) *Replay of the assertion from another device* — refused: bound to the device credential (ADR-0007 Validation criteria, F120c). (8) *Stolen phone session* — the attacker needs a fresh second factor for the approval (step-up). (9) *Request after the owner's click* — an attacker who triggers a fresh request when the victim clicks defeats a short lifetime (RFC 10027 § 6.1.2, F121); the consent screen, not the lifetime, is the control. (10) *A second approver races the first* — one pending request, one approval (compare-and-set).

### Part 2 — 5. Decision

All four are **Recommended - pending user decision**. The founder decides; `cto` records any ADR-0007 amendment. Every number below is a proposed configuration default: no source gives it (F208, F225). Section 4 headings are final (the "provisional" tag there is lifted).

**Decision 1 — notice threshold N. Recommended - pending user decision: option 1C.**
- Count consecutive wrong passwords on a *known* login since its last success (NIST resets on success, F207). At N send one owner notice, at most one per rolling window per login. **Proposed defaults N = 10, window 24 h, both configuration; the numbers are not sourced.** Add a notice on a correct password that follows N or more failures (ASVS 6.3.5 example, F202). The 100-failure block notice already decided in M2 stays the third tier.
- Why: OWASP's default is no notice for a plain wrong password and a notice for "password right, factor wrong" (F209); the only peer-reviewed data says notices for every event cause fatigue and that plain wording with device, time and a next step works best (F225, F210; successful logins only, failures not studied). 1A alone misses the success-after-failures case; 1B alone never reports an attacker who never succeeds.
- Notice (a) carries no action link (a phishing-lookalike risk, T7.1); the "not me" link stays only on the second-factor-pending notice. A one-guess-per-account spray never reaches N; that is caught by the aggregate alert (M50), not the owner notice.
- ADR needed: amendment to ADR-0007 § 3 (one line).

**Decision 2 — challenge. Recommended - pending user decision: option 2A now (no challenge), with 2B (ALTCHA MIT + `altcha-lib-java` MIT) designed as a triggered fallback, not built.**
- 2A: refuse before any hash (429 with `Retry-After`), a bounded Argon2 queue with a wait deadline, and a **reserved permit lane for requests carrying the known-device signal** so a flood cannot starve owners (design inference). A refusal costs microseconds against about 13 ms per verify on the test host (F219, host only; S13 PENDING - S13).
- Why not PoW now: a hash-search PoW does not price out a botnet (F219, F224 lead, F209), it taxes low-end phones (a 2023 mid-range phone took 2.5 s for 100,000 cycles, F227; Egyptian handsets NOT SOURCEABLE; S23 PENDING - S23), vendor accessibility claims are unverified (F213), and WebView support is untested.
- Trigger to build 2B: S13/M43 shows the cap alone fails, or the credential-stuffing alert fires. Then challenge only above a configured load threshold, signed single-use expiring challenges.
- Turnstile (2C) ranks last: it sends client IP, TLS fingerprint, User-Agent and sitekey/origin to a vendor that states no processing region or retention (F215). The Egyptian transfer text is ti- F511; reported, not interpreted. mCaptcha (2D) is AGPL-3.0 and idle since 2024 (F211); licence-blocked for any customer bundle, listed only.
- ADR needed: no new ADR unless 2B is triggered.

**Decision 3 — breached-password source. Recommended - pending user decision: 3A plus 3C.**
- HIBP range API from the backend only: SHA-1 computed in memory, **only the 5-hex prefix leaves**, `Add-Padding: true`, suffix compared locally (F204, F220). Plus a bundled top-100k or top-1M list as the always-on floor for ASVS 6.2.4 (F222; per-file provenance NOT SOURCEABLE, check before shipping). Timeout, circuit breaker, reply size cap, prefix cache (F206 shows 31-day cache headers).
- Offline corpus (3B) not now: about 81 GB by estimate (F206), vendor gives no size or cadence (F205, F220), over the 100 MB cap so not fetched.
- Fail-open or fail-closed when HIBP is unreachable is **the founder's call** (no SLA, F220). My proposal: fail open on the local list, write a `breach_check_skipped` event, and recheck at the next successful sign-in while the plaintext is in hand.
- ADR needed: amendment to ADR-0007 § 2 password policy.

**Decision 4 — throttle state across restarts. Recommended - pending user decision: narrow 4C (a logged PostgreSQL table) plus memory.**
- Persist, in PostgreSQL, the per-known-login consecutive-failure count, the ceiling block and the "not me" block. The blocks are durable account state (M2: "until a reset"), so memory cannot hold them whatever else is chosen (inference from F221). Keep per-address delays and unknown-identifier state in memory: the address delay never alone blocks a known identifier (M2), so its loss on restart is bounded (T1.19, T10.7).
- Writes happen only after admission control and only for existing logins, so an attacker cannot grow the table with invented identifiers. Rows are keyed by the throttle HMAC and expire. At a second instance the per-login state is already shared; only per-address state then needs a shared store (Valkey/Redis, F216).
- Why not the others: rebuild from security events (4B) cannot recover per-address state (no full IPs) and may lack an id (F221); UNLOGGED (4D) loses the table on a Postgres crash (F218); Redis (4E) adds a service and persistence is off by default (F216). Write cost is untimed: **PENDING - S24**.
- ADR needed: amendment to ADR-0007 § 3 (replaces "lost on restart, accepted" for per-login state).

**Proposed pin changes:** none. ALTCHA, HIBP and the bundled list are not pinned; propose pins only if built.

Repository level: L2 generally, L3 for auth (staff-auth-note F127). OWASP API Top 10 2023: API2, API4, API6 (F228).
- **Advisories (searched 2026-10-19 via OSV, S22; GHSA not queried separately):** none for `bcprov-jdk18on` 1.86, `altcha` 3.4.1, `altcha-lib` 2.6.0, `@cap.js/widget` 0.1.58, `@cap.js/server` 4.0.5; control query returned 7 (F212). Not searched: `altcha-lib-java` 2.1.1, HIBP (a service), Turnstile (a service). NOT SOURCEABLE - budget, not reached.
- **Abuse cases:**
  1. Spray wrong passwords at an owner to trigger notices (email bombing, mail reputation): one notice per window per login; no action link in notice (a).
  2. Flood sign-ins to fill the Argon2 queue and lock owners out: admission before hash, reserved lane for known-device attempts, passkey path needs no Argon2 (T1.17, T1.18).
  3. A flood that exhausts memory crashes the JVM and clears in-memory throttle state: persisted per-login counts and blocks (T1.19, T10.7).
  4. A tampered or hostile reply from the range API (huge, malformed, all-hit): TLS, size cap, timeout, parse strictly; reply does not decide anything beyond accept or refuse a password.
  5. HIBP or its CDN sees the 20-bit prefix and our server's IP at the moment a password is set: the password and full hash never leave (T1.26); linking a prefix to an account is not possible from the request alone (inference). Deliberately no jitter or batching proposed.
  6. If 2B is built: replayed or pre-solved challenges; require HMAC-signed, expiring, single-use challenges.
  7. Write amplification: attacker-chosen identifiers must not create rows (known logins only).
- **Threat model:** `docs/architecture/threat-models/staff-auth.md` (M22, M2, M3, M43).

### Part 2 — 7. Performance & Caching Notes

- **Expected load:** password set events are rare (HIBP calls tens per day per product at most; estimate, no source). Failed sign-ins are attacker-controlled, so budget for a flood, not the average. One Argon2id verify at the floor: 13.3 ms median and about 19 MiB on the test host (F219); VPS value **PENDING - S13**; memory ceiling is cap n times that plus JVM overhead.
- **Hot paths:** sign-in admission check (must stay allocation-light, no hash), Argon2 queue, per-login throttle UPSERT (untimed, **PENDING - S24**), HIBP call only off the sign-in path (at set time, plus the proposed recheck).
- **Cache decision the throttle state needs (gate 5, `caching-standard.md`):** the pattern is *in-process store plus durable per-login row*. Key = throttle HMAC of identifier or address (never raw); TTL = decay window for delays, and the row's expiry for counts; invalidation = success resets the count, a password reset clears the block and the known-device cookie hash; no stampede issue (writes are per key); never cache a verification result or a "password is fine" answer. The per-address delay is the only memory-only part.
- **Cache decision for the HIBP reply:** in-process LRU by 5-hex prefix, public data, TTL proposed 24 h (the service sends 31 days, F206), single-flight per prefix to stop a stampede, bounded entries (about 77 KB each, F206).
- **Budgets affected:** `perf-budgets.md` has no sign-in or Argon2 budget (grep of the file found none); until set it is "not checked", never "met". Counter budgets are unaffected (the counter never waits).
