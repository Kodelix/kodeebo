## 1. Question

ADR-0014 must fix one catalogue and item core that serves every Kodeebo vertical (café, gaming now; restaurant and retail later), designed complete once, with tiers deciding only what the UI exposes (founder, 2026-10-20). The research asked, area by area, how established systems model each part of that core and which shape Kodeebo should take: (1) categories, items, variants and sizes, retail variant attributes, barcode and SKU per variant; (2) Arabic and English text on every entity and what a receipt prints when a language is missing; (3) modifiers and modifier groups (required, minimum and maximum, defaults, priced or free, nesting, stock effect); (4) combos and bundles and counter quick-button presets; (5) price per branch and per order type, availability per branch ("sold out today") and menu scheduling; (6) the fields that carry the founder's tax, service and minimum-charge hierarchy (structure only); (7) units of measure with conversion (purchase, stock, recipe unit); (8) recipes per variant with sub-recipes, yield and wastage, waste, and stock on void; (9) where station time and station price tiers sit (item kind 1); (10) the line between Catalogue and Inventory inside the one model and the module dependency direction under ADR-0010 and ADR-0011. Sources: Square, Toast, Shopify, Lightspeed (US/EU POS), Odoo and ERPNext (ERP, units and BOM), gaming-lounge and billiard products (public pages), and the regional systems Foodics (Saudi, Arabic-first) and Loyverse (common in Egyptian cafés), added at the founder's request. Constraints in force: item kinds 1 to 5; Catalogue owns the sellable side and the item-to-stock or item-to-recipe link, Inventory the stock side; tax and service values are owned by Tax & Receipts; integer piasters (ADR-0006); offline counters fed by the sync feed; End Day closes the business day; Arabic and RTL first (P-LOC); P-VERTICAL; the counter 3 s ceiling.

## 5. Decision

**Decided by the founder, 2026-10-21** (answers given to the main session after the parts returned; `cto` records them in ADR-0014, which stays `Proposed` until accepted together with ADR-0010 to ADR-0013 after the one independent review over ADR-0001 to ADR-0014).

**D1 — Basis.** The recommended model of § 4 is the basis for ADR-0014; D2 to D12 adjust the named parts only. In short: a category tree with **one category per item**; every item has at least one **variant** (SKU, barcodes unique per tenant, price rows, the stock or recipe link); tenant-level **option axes**; brand on the item; **translation rows** for every person-readable catalogue string; **modifier groups** attached per item with min, max, defaults, `free_options`, `unique_options`, `excluded_options`, nesting allowed in the model, option prices and availability per branch, and a stock effect as an Inventory-owned **recipe delta**; **bundles** with slots, sizes and upgradeable slots, sold as a parent line with child lines that keep their own kind; **presets** as stored selections; **base price on the variant with sparse override rows** by order type (tenant-wide) and by branch (branch-scoped); **availability** rows per branch (not offered; sold out today stamped with the business-day id, so End Day ends it; stock-driven sold-out signalled by Inventory); one **shared schedule value type** (weekdays, local time window, optional date range, optional order-type and branch sets) owned by the money and time rules ADR; **availability is a gate, price is a cascade**; Catalogue holds **no tax, service or minimum-charge data** (Tax & Receipts keys its rules by branch, order type, category and item ids); **units** in categories with integer quantities in a reference unit and a whole-number flag per unit, pack units per supplier with an item default; **recipes** per variant, versioned, output quantity and wastage per line, sub-recipes phantom or stocked per sub-recipe, flattened at publish; one **movement ledger** written with the bill line on the device, waste with a mandatory reason and its own permission, voids reversing the stored movements; **station time** as a catalogue item with selling method `time` while tiers, rules and the meter stay in Gaming (A3) and one set of pure price functions lives in the money and time kernel (R2); **Catalogue depends on Inventory** (B1) and owns the link rows, with one feed item per context (F2).

**D2 — Price precedence.** When a price is set at several levels, the most specific wins in this order: branch **and** order type, then order type alone, then branch alone, then the variant's base price. A bill line freezes the price and its provenance (matched level and row) when it is added.

**D3 — Time-based price changes are G9.** A catalogue price has no time dimension and no percentage. A happy-hour or weekday price change is a G9 rule through the one discount model (as a discount or a surcharge), never a catalogue price layer. **In reports, G9 automatic time-based pricing is its own category, separate from cashier discounts**, so a morning rate never reads as staff discounting.

**D4 — Combo pricing mode per combo.** Each combo picks one of two modes: one combo price split across its child lines by a fixed rule (largest remainder in piasters; spike S20 PENDING), or owner-set prices per slot option, per combo size and per branch (the Foodics shape, part-8 F840). A combo price is never a discount.

**D5 — Stock at void or refund.** Per voided or refunded line: back to stock, or waste with a reason. Default: return for a line not yet made, waste once the line is marked made. Both are movement facts in the one ledger (part-8 F887 is the regional precedent).

**D6 — Open price and sold by weight are attributes of ordinary items; kind 5 stays separate.** Any normal catalogue item may be **open price** (the cashier types the price, within the role's limit and under the permission rules already decided) or **sold by weight** (a price per kilogram or per unit of weight; the cashier enters the weight; line amount = weight × unit price; stock is deducted in the base unit, grams). Example: fish at 180 EGP per kg. The **custom item (kind 5)** stays one seeded item for things not in the catalogue, with the typed name and price on the bill line. **Recorded in the model now, implemented later with retail:** weight- or price-embedded barcodes printed by label scales (EAN-13 starting with 2), so a scanned label yields the item and its weight or price. Scale hardware integration is a later feature, outside ADR-0014's scope.

**D7 — Languages.** Translation rows keyed by an open language tag; one primary language per tenant, mandatory on every entity; the UI exposes Arabic and English only; a third language later needs no migration. The receipt language is a branch setting; a name missing in the receipt language falls back to the primary language and is never blank (R1).

**D8 — Rounding.** Per branch: mode none, nearest, up or down, plus a step in piasters; applied once per bill; the rounding amount is stored as its own line and shown on the receipt. The arithmetic rule belongs to the money and time rules ADR.

**D9 — Station tiers carry one base rate.** A tier has one base rate with no time-of-day dimension; a cheaper or dearer time of day is a G9 rule on top (D3). Rate-replacing session rules (fixed price, free minutes, unit price) are part of the meter's computation and recorded as provenance.

**D10 — Station time in a combo is prepaid time.** Selling a combo such as "one hour of play plus a drink" creates prepaid time that the next or current session on that bill consumes; the meter charges only the overtime at the tier rate (S50 case 6). Unused prepaid time is not refunded and expires at End Day; the owner may change that per combo later.

**D11 — A tier change mid-session gives one bill line per segment** (tier, start, end, seconds, amount), each rounded once (S50 case 2).

**D12 — An overnight window belongs to the day it started.** A Thursday 20:00–02:00 window includes Friday 00:00–02:00; this matches a business day closed by End Day, not by midnight.

**Not decided here (owners in § 9):** which Orders event deducts stock; kind 2 strictly one-to-one or a quantity per sale; recipe scaling across sizes; the cost method (moving average or FIFO) and the loss of a stocked batch; recipe lines excluded by order type (R-b recommended); the permission keys for price overrides, sold-out toggles, waste and void disposition; concurrent offline availability toggles; the End Day reminder; lounge practices (controller-count bands, block rounding, extras modes); the barcode namespaces for sale and pack barcodes; where the negative-stock policy lives; named tax groups as a rule value and a tax registration per branch.

**Why the others lost:** each part's § 5 in the appendices gives the reasons per area. In short: flat items, column pairs and per-vertical copies break "designed complete once"; a combo as a discount or a time-based catalogue price layer duplicates the one discount model; wall-clock sold-out disagrees with End Day; free-text recurrence strings need a parser on the counter; decimals and floats drift (S40, S50); a meter in Catalogue gives a vertical-agnostic module a station dimension and a second rule engine; Inventory depending on Catalogue moves the founder's link or leaves it unconstrained.

### 5. Decision

**Chosen**: Recommended - pending user decision. Area A: option A1. Area B: option B4 with receipt rule R1. Both go into ADR-0014.

**What A1 means for the ADR** (proposal, vendor models compared, none copied):
1. Categories: a self-referencing tree per tenant with an ordinal among siblings (as Square `parent_category`, F108). **One category per item**, because the tax hierarchy takes one category id (F132); extra groupings (quick-button presets, menus) are separate structures in parts 2 and 3. Maximum depth is a config value, not a constant; Square states none (F108).
2. Item: carries the **kind** (1, 2, 3, 5; kind 4 stays in Inventory, per the founder rule), the category, the archived flag and its translations. No tax, service or minimum-charge field (F102 shows what to avoid). Kind 5 (open item): proposed as one seeded system item per tenant, with the typed name and price living on the bill line; this is Open question 6.
3. Variant: every item has at least one (a default variant for simple items, as Square requires, F102). The variant holds SKU, its barcodes, the stock-item link (kind 2) or recipe link (kind 3) and is the key for price rows and recipes. A check ties the link to the item's kind.
4. Options: tenant-level option definitions (Size, Colour, ...) with translated values; a variant picks one value per axis; the variant display label is derived from the item name and the option value labels (as Square and Lightspeed do, F103, F111), so there is no second free text to translate. The number of axes and variants per item are config values; comparators: Square 6 axes and 250 variants (F102, F103), Lightspeed 3 axes and 200 variants (F111), Shopify 2,048 variants (F106).
5. Brand: an item-level reference (translatable name), not an axis, following Lightspeed where brand sits on the parent (F111). Colour and size are axes. Open question 2.
6. Barcodes: a separate table, many per variant (Shopify 20, F106; Lightspeed several, F112), each with a validated type, stored as text so leading zeroes survive, **unique per tenant** so a scan resolves to exactly one variant. SKU unique per tenant; case rule for the spec.

**What B4 means for the ADR**:
1. Every catalogue string a person reads (item, description, category, option name, option value, brand, modifier group, modifier) lives in a translation row keyed by (entity id, locale). Nothing in resource files (F131).
2. The tenant has one primary locale; a translation in it is mandatory for every entity (enforced in the domain and by a constraint), so a resolution chain always ends. The schema allows any language tag; the UI exposes Arabic and English only (tiers decide the UI, not the model).
3. Receipt: Tax & Receipts picks the receipt language; each printed name resolves by R1. The bill line stores the printed name as sold, so renaming an item later does not rewrite old bills (a design proposal; the Orders owner confirms).
4. Text is plain text. No HTML (Square allows limited HTML in descriptions, F101; not copied).
5. A `short_name` for narrow receipt columns and counter buttons is optional (Toast `posName`, Square `abbreviation`, F101, F109 show the need); kitchen-ticket names are left to kitchen printing (Open question 9).

**Why the others lost**: A2 breaks the founder rule and forces a later model change. A3 and A4 push family and tax rules into code and fit an online store (A4) or a grouped name (A3) less well than the tax-by-category rule needs. B1 is not "designed complete once" and repeats a migration per language. B2 loses foreign keys and complicates RLS and sync. B3 weakens constraints and Arabic search. R2 puts a failure on the cashier; R3 is a layout choice that can sit on top of R1 later.

**Not decided here**: station time and price tiers (part 5, F136), modifiers (part 2), combos, scheduling, price and availability rows, recipes and units.

**Follow-up record**: ADR-0014 (yes). Spikes S10 and S11 are PENDING; the decision does not rest on them, but the counter search design (S10) and the feed sizing (S11) do.

### 5. Decision

**Chosen**: Recommended - pending user decision (decided by `cto` in ADR-0014, the founder choosing): **A1 + B1 + S1 + C1**, with the rules below.

**The recommended model, in plain terms (indicative names; ADR-0014 names them):**

1. **Price (A1).** The variant holds the base price (integer piasters, ADR-0006). A modifier option and a combo hold prices the same way (Q9). Two override tables hold fixed prices only: tenant-wide `(variant, order type id)` and branch-scoped `(branch id, variant, order type id or NULL)`. The tenant-wide table has no `branch_id`, the branch table has a non-null one, so each fits the ADR-0003 branch policy unchanged (F315). Resolution is a pure function in Catalogue: `resolve(variant, branch id, order type id) -> price + which level matched + the row id`. The caller (Orders) supplies both ids; Catalogue never imports Orders.
2. **Which level wins.** Most specific wins, like the tax hierarchy: (branch AND order type) first, then order type alone, then branch alone, then base. That order follows the founder's chain "branch default, then order type" where order type is the more specific level (F314); it is a recommendation and the founder must confirm it (Q1). No vendor page read shows a two-axis price, so this rule has no vendor evidence behind it (F322).
3. **The line between price and G9.** A catalogue price has no time dimension and no percentage. Time-of-day or day-of-week price changes (happy hour) and percentage uplifts go through the one discount model or a surcharge, not through catalogue prices (F314). Square keeps time-based price changes in pricing rules separate from the item price (F304); Toast puts them inside the item's price strategy (F313): Kodeebo follows Square's separation because the founder drew that line.
4. **Price is frozen when the line is added.** Resolve returns the matched level and row id; the bill line stores the piasters and that provenance, so a later price edit never reprices an open bill, and the offline device and the server can be compared in review.
5. **Availability (B1).** One branch-scoped table per sellable variant (and modifier option, Q9): state `not_offered` (until changed) or `sold_out_today` (carries the business day id; valid only while the caller's current business day id equals it). Absence of a row means offered. Setting it, and the End Day reset, need no job. Stock-linked automatic sold-out (kind 2 and 3 items reaching zero) is a signal from Inventory through a port, not stored here (Q8).
6. **Scheduling (S1).** One shared schedule value type (weekday set, local start and end, optional date range, several windows allowed) attached to a menu and to an item. It should be one type owned outside Catalogue because G9 rules and the G11 minimum-charge schedule need the same shape (Toast reuses one Schedule for menu availability and for time-specific price, F312-F313); the money and time rules ADR is the natural owner (Q5).
7. **Availability is a gate, price is a cascade.** Values (price, tax) take the most specific match. Gates (offered at branch, not sold out today, item schedule passes, at least one containing menu schedule passes) all have to pass: a failed gate anywhere makes the item unavailable. State this once in ADR-0014 so the two rules are not mixed.
8. **Hierarchy structure (C1).** Tax & Receipts owns one rule table keyed by `(scope kind, scope id)` with scope kinds branch, order type, category, item (sellable or stock item), plus an optional branch qualifier where a level can differ per branch (Q7). Catalogue's obligations: stable ids; **each sellable item belongs to exactly one category**; categories form a tree with one parent each, and Catalogue can return an item's ancestor list in order (Q6 decides whether a parent's rule reaches subcategories); variants share their item's rule. Menus and counter quick-button presets are separate groupings that never carry tax data. Catalogue has no tax, service or minimum-charge column.

**Why**: A1 gives the two-axis price the founder asked for with the smallest model that resolves offline in a bounded number of lookups, and keeps one item per sale (reporting, stock, recipes and barcodes stay single). B1 matches the founder's own statement that End Day, not the clock, closes the day, and needs no job or timestamp policy. S1 covers time of day, day of week and date range without a recurrence parser on the counter. C1 keeps tax values where the domain map says they live and keeps Catalogue free of rule data.

**Why not the others**: A2's strengths are percentage adjustments and reusable lists, but it adds list precedence and rounding rules and blurs price versus surcharge; revisit it only if Q10 says owners want a delivery uplift as a catalogue concept. A3 is the simplest mental model for an owner and the cheapest lookup, but it duplicates items. B2 is simplest and vendor-proven (Square) but wall-clock based. B3 couples End Day to Catalogue. S2 is the most expressive and standards-based, but expressiveness beyond the founder's list is a cost on the counter. C2 gives the nicest editing screen but breaks ownership. C3 (named taxes on items) is complementary, not a substitute.

**Follow-up record**: ADR-0014 (item model), yes. It must cite this note and the other parts of the series. The money and time rules ADR (Still-Missing item 6) must decide Q5 and Q10's rounding side.

### 5. Decision

**Chosen**: Recommended - pending user decision (decided by `cto` in ADR-0014, the founder choosing): **A1 + B4 + C1 + D**, with the rules below. Vendor models compared; none copied; no Foodics comparison was possible (F423).

**The recommended model, in plain terms (indicative names; ADR-0014 names them):**

1. **Units (A1).** `uom_category` and `uom` are tenant data with seeded metric defaults; a unit belongs to one category and has an exact factor to the category's reference unit. Who may edit a unit already used: nobody; a used unit is archived, not changed (history stays readable).
2. **Where each unit is attached.** *Stock item:* one **stock unit** (what is counted and shown) in some category; quantity on hand is stored as an integer of the category's reference unit. *Purchase:* the stock item lists its purchase units (unit, factor to the stock unit, optional pack barcode), as ERPNext and Odoo do per item (F402, F411); the **purchase line** stores the unit entered, the quantity entered and the factor, then converts to the stock unit on receipt. *Recipe line:* its own unit, which must be in the stock item's category (Odoo's same-category rule, F402); stores the entered quantity and unit and the converted integer. *Sellable variant:* kind 2 sells one stock unit per sale and kind 3 goes through a recipe. Anything else, such as five glasses from a bottle, is a one-line recipe, so Square's separate stockable conversion (part-1 F104) is not copied (Open question 3).
3. **Precision and rounding.** No floats anywhere. Money stays integer piasters (ADR-0006). Stock value is carried as a running value per stock item and branch, and each consumption takes its share by rounding half-up while the remainder stays in the value, so cost out plus value left always equals cost in (S40 case 3). Rounding to the reference unit happens once per movement. A non-whole conversion into a count unit is rejected at entry with a clear message, or rounded by an owner-set rule (Open question 6). Whether the average is moving average or FIFO belongs to the money-and-time rules ADR; ERPNext offers both (F411).
4. **Recipes (B4).** A recipe belongs to a variant (founder rule), has an output quantity, lines (stock item or prepared item, quantity, unit, wastage in basis points), an acyclic check, a configurable maximum depth, and versions. A recipe may be in **draft**: an item whose recipe is draft still sells and records a flagged "no consumption" fact (Toast shows draft recipes do not drive depletion, F413, lead). Recipe cost is derived and recomputed (F408), never typed.
5. **Modifiers.** An option's stock effect is a recipe delta owned by Inventory (part-2 A3: add, replace, remove an ingredient quantity); the sale writes base-recipe movements plus delta movements, each tagged with the option id.
6. **Consumption and void (C1).** The device computes the movements locally from the synced recipe version and writes them in the same local transaction as the bill line; the server accepts them as facts, flags negative results, applies the per-item policy decided on the device (F420), and flags a line whose recipe version is older than the server's (as the config-version drift flag in the sync note). A void writes reversing movements copied from the stored ones, idempotent on the original movement id. A made-then-voided item should be waste, not a return (Open question 1).
7. **Waste (D).** `waste` is a movement type with a mandatory reason from the reason list, plus `count_adjustment`, `receive`, `sale`, `void_return`, `production_in`, `production_out`, `transfer`. Theoretical use is the sum of `sale` and `void_return` movements; actual use is that plus waste and count adjustments between two counts; the difference is the variance. Stock is per branch; a movement table carries `tenant_id`, `branch_id` and the branch RLS policy (part-3 F315).
8. **Names.** Stock items, units and prepared items get translation rows like the other catalogue entities (part-1 B4). Arabic-first display of ingredients and units is a design consequence of the P-LOC rule; no Gulf or Egypt vendor practice could be sourced (F423).

**Why**: integers in a reference unit are exact, identical in PostgreSQL and the counter's store, and cheap (S40 cases 1, 2, 5, 6); categories give the validation the brief asks for (F402); keeping both sub-recipe modes in one model lets a Tier 1 café start with no batch logging and later track batches without a migration (F405, F410); the ledger of movement facts is what the sync design already assumes (F420), and one ledger gives waste, void return, receipt and counts a common variance query.

**Why not the others**: A2 repeats the rounding problem with a heavier type and an unverified device representation; A3 allows nonsense conversions. B1 fails "sub-recipes"; B2 alone hides batch waste; B3 alone burdens a Tier 1 café. C2 makes the void rule vacuous (F421); C3 cannot support live stock, sold-out or refuse policy. A separate waste table (D2) splits the variance query.

**Conditions and flags**: S40 is arithmetic on illustrative numbers; PostgreSQL 18.6 and SQLite behaviour is PENDING (S41), and counter flatten timing is PENDING (S42). The vendor comparison for Toast, Lightspeed and Square rests on search summaries (F412 to F415, leads): do not cite them in the ADR before the pages are read. Foodics, Odoo scrap and rounding, and the ERPNext UOM page are NOT SOURCEABLE.

**Follow-up record**: ADR-0014 (item model) - yes. Likely also an ADR or section for the stock movement ledger if `stock-core` is treated as an architectural decision; `cto` decides. **Proposed pin changes**: none.

### 5. Decision

**Chosen**: Recommended - pending user decision (decided by `cto` in ADR-0014, the founder choosing): **A3 with R2** for station time, **B1 with F2** for the Catalogue/Inventory line. Vendor models compared; none copied. Nothing here changes what parts 1 to 4, 6 to 8 recommend except where stated.

#### 5.1 Station time and tiers (A3 + R2), what it means for ADR-0014

1. **The sellable item is a catalogue item; the meter is Gaming's.** Each time product is a kind-1 item with a category, AR/EN names, availability and schedule gates, presets and combo slots like any item. Its **selling method is `time`** (extends part-8 K2: unit, weight, now time); the quantity is a whole number of the method's reference unit (seconds), stored as an integer as part-4 A1 does. Catalogue stores no unit table for this (the reference unit follows from the method), so it needs no dependency on Inventory's units. A `time` item has no catalogue price rows (validated); a rate is not catalogue data. The selling method of an item that has been sold is immutable.
2. **Tiers, rules and the meter stay in Gaming**, where the founder's ownership table already puts "station price tiers" (F501): rate per hour by station type and controller-count band (single, multi), the one-time fixed fee, minimum minutes, block rounding, frozen prices, tier change, rules with priority, stackable flag, maximum uses per session and validity dates, extras modes (free, flat per session, per hour, included in the tier, with included and maximum quantity per station type) and the end-of-time alert. A tier row names the catalogue item id it is sold as. Variants as tiers (A3-variant) lose because a tier changes inside a session and a variant is a sold unit with SKU and barcode.
3. **Who computes a time line: Gaming, on the device, offline.** It sends Orders the line (catalogue item id, seconds, amount in integer piasters, provenance: tier id and version, rule ids, segments, opaque `source` id). Orders checks the item's kind and method against Catalogue, asks Tax & Receipts to resolve tax with the item's category id and item id and order type "session" (F505), and records the amount as a money fact: never rejected, flagged if the server's recomputation differs. Catalogue's price resolution is used only for flat-fee kind-1 items, never for the meter. No port from Catalogue or Orders to Gaming is needed: this is the existing "Gaming depends on Orders; opaque `source`" pattern (F502), plus a Gaming-to-Catalogue read.
4. **No second pricing engine (R2).** The pure functions live in the money-and-time kernel and are used by the meter, the discount model and the resolver; S50 is the reason they must be shared: rounding mode and float versus exact (F540), once-per-line rounding (F541, F544) and a total order of rules (F543). Rate-replacing rules (fixed price, free minutes, unit price) are part of the meter's computation and carried as provenance; rules that reduce or add by day of week or time of day (G9) reach the bill as `Discounts`-port entries. Base tiers carry no time-of-day dimension. This boundary is part-8 Q1 and is **not decided here**; R3 is the fallback if the founder wants every reduction in Orders.
5. **Combos ("one hour of play plus a drink").** The slot names the time item and a quantity of 3,600 s. Selling the bundle creates a child line for the time component (its price from the bundle's pricing mode; for a time slot the author-set per-slot price of part-8 L2 avoids asking Gaming for a list price) that acts as **prepaid time** for a later session; the meter charges only the overtime at the tier rate (S50 case 6: 4,800 s against 3,600 s included gives 1,200 s charged 2,000 piasters at 6,000/h). Whether the sale itself starts a session is open (Q5). Part-2's allocation rule S20 is still PENDING.
6. **Extras and accessories.** Each chargeable extra and the lost-item charge is a kind-1 catalogue item with an ordinary price or a per-hour amount computed by the meter; the unit's state (kind 4) changes in Inventory through the accessories port called by Gaming. A line is "unit price x quantity x time, rounded once per line and snapshotted" (F544).
7. **Restaurant and Retail.** They ignore it (default method `unit`; no table or screen appears, the UI is hidden by tier) or reuse the same pattern for a metered thing (room hire, equipment rental) by writing their own meter that sends lines to Orders; the Catalogue change is the same one attribute.

**Why A3**: the founder already placed tiers and sessions in Gaming (F501); tax, names, category, combos and reports need a catalogue item (F505, F506, part-2 B3); every gaming product read has the timer owner compute the amount (F520); and putting the meter in Catalogue (A1) would give a vertical-agnostic module a station dimension, a session-state price and a second rule engine (F504). **Why not the others**: A1 as above, and Odoo's whole-period model is not pro rata (F514). A2 collapses into A3 once tax, names and combos are met (F505, F506). A3-variant duplicates tier ownership. A4 is a pack model that a bundle already expresses.

#### 5.2 Catalogue / Inventory (B1 + F2), what it means for ADR-0014

1. **Who owns what.** *Catalogue:* categories, items and their kind, variants, option axes and values, barcodes and SKU, modifier groups and options with attachments, bundles and slots, presets, base and override prices, availability rows, schedules, translations of all these, and the **link rows**: variant to stock item (kind 2, one stock unit per sale), variant to recipe (kind 3, unique per variant), option to recipe delta. *Inventory:* unit categories and units, stock items with purchase units and supplier packs, recipes (versions, lines, output quantity, wastage in basis points, prepared items phantom or stocked), recipe deltas, the movement ledger (sale, void return, waste with reason, count adjustment, receive, production, transfer), stock per branch, the negative-stock policy, purchases, accessory units (kind 4) and the translations of its own entities. Kind 4 is never in a link.
2. **Direction.** Catalogue depends on Inventory; Inventory depends on neither Catalogue, Orders nor Gaming. Reasons: the sellable side names the stock side in all four vendors read (F535); the founder's wording "Catalogue owns the link, Inventory owns the recipes" (F501) is only expressible with Catalogue pointing at Inventory without a cycle (F538); Inventory stays usable alone for purchasing, counts and retail receiving. A recipe is therefore a stand-alone definition and "recipe per variant" is a unique constraint on Catalogue's link. A shared base recipe with a size multiplier (part-4 Q4) would live in the link row.
3. **Foreign keys and Exposed.** Catalogue's link tables carry the Inventory id as a plain column with the SQL foreign key `(tenant_id, id)`; no Exposed `reference()` across modules; Inventory migrations run before Catalogue's (ADR-0010 Decision 5 order). ADR-0010's wording names Layer 1 for the foreign-key direction (F508): ADR-0014 states the same rule for Layer 2 pairs, and `architecture-rules.properties` gains a line that Inventory never depends on Catalogue. Layer 2 modules carry no ABI dump (ADR-0010), so the two port sets need contract tests of their own.
4. **Ports** (indicative names; ADR-0014 names them). Defined by Inventory (`inventory.app.port`): `StockItemCatalog` (exists, active, unit category), `RecipeCatalog` (current version, draft state, flattened list with chosen deltas applied), `StockConsumption` (record sale consumption for a bill line; reverse by copying the stored movements, part-4 C1), `StockLevels` (is an item depleted at a branch, its policy). Defined by Catalogue (`catalogue.app.port`): `SellableCatalog` (variant to kind, names, category id, price with caller-passed branch, order type and business-day ids) and `StockLinkQuery` (variant and chosen options to the resolved stock effect: stock item, or recipe id plus delta ids). Orders and the counter call both; Inventory calls neither.
5. **The modifier delta crossing.** The option row (Catalogue) holds the delta id; the delta (Inventory) is a list of absolute changes (stock item, quantity in the reference unit, add, replace or remove) that does not depend on a base recipe, so "oat milk instead of dairy" is one remove and one add. A sale writes base-recipe movements plus delta movements, each tagged with the option id (part-4 section 5). A recipe line's optional order-type exclusion set (part-8 R-b) stores order type ids as opaque values.
6. **Automatic sold-out.** The manual "sold out today" row stays in Catalogue (part-3 B1). Stock-driven unavailability is the availability gate calling `StockLevels` (Catalogue to Inventory, allowed), evaluated on the device from the local stock sum under the item's policy (part-4 F420); Inventory never writes into Catalogue.
7. **Feed (F2).** As in section 4.2; the sale line records the recipe version used and drift is flagged (part-4). Under F2 the counter composes the stock effect locally at add-line from the two local copies.

**Why B1**: it is the only option that keeps the founder's link ownership, the Gradle-direction rule for foreign keys and a database-enforced link (F538, F508). **Why not the others**: B2 moves the link or leaves it unconstrained. B3 amends ADR-0011 and removes the compile wall the founder chose. B4 loses integrity across the line. F1 couples Sync to both contexts' internals.

**Conditions and flags**: the vendor evidence for time-billed services is marketing and FAQ pages (tier 5) and two tier-2 pages that model time as a fixed duration or whole periods (F512-F514); the recommendation rests on the founder's ownership table (F501) and the module rules (F507, F508), not on vendor practice. S50 is arithmetic on illustrative numbers; S51 and S52 are PENDING (F546), so no timing and no database behaviour backs the dependency direction yet. ADR-0010 and ADR-0011 are Proposed, so the module paths used here may change if they do.

**Follow-up record**: ADR-0014 (yes, already planned); the money-and-time rules ADR (Still-Missing item 6) must fix the line rounding mode, the rule order and the shared functions of R2. **Proposed pin changes**: none.

#### 5.4 Recommendation changes (only where the evidence changes it)

1. **Modifiers (part-2 A3)**: add to the per-attachment settings `free_options`, `unique_options` and `excluded_options` (part-6 Y-a, now tier 2), keep `default_options`; treat `is_splittable_in_half` as a later flag. Give modifier options the same per-branch price, active and in-stock rows as variants (part-3 Q9, answered yes).
2. **Combos (part-2 B3)**: add bundle **sizes** and an `is_upgradable` slot flag, and offer a **pricing mode** per bundle: allocate one bundle price (L1) or author-set per-slot-option prices per size and branch (L2), i.e. option L3. Part-6 Y-b is now sourced at tier 2. S20 stays PENDING for the allocation mode.
3. **Part-3 F322** is corrected: Foodics prices by order type. The decision A1 stands (it already prices by order type); the claim "none was seen" no longer holds.
4. **Schedules (part-3 S1)**: the shared schedule value type gains an optional order-type set and optional branch set; part-3 recommendation 3 is kept but flagged (option G3 preferred, Q1).
5. **Rounding (founder rule)**: add a step (in piasters) and a "none" mode, applied once per bill, with the rounding amount kept as its own bill line and a receipt-visibility setting. Pending the founder.
6. **Recipes (part-4)**: cost mode fixed or derived (C-b), replacing "never typed"; optional order-type exclusion set on recipe lines (R-b, pending Q6); pack unit per supplier with an item default (P3); `quantity per sale` for kind 2 is supported (part-4 Q3).
7. **Void stock (part-4 Q1)**: per-line choice of return or waste (V2), default return, pending the founder.
8. **Kind 5 (part-1 Q6)**: K2, pending the founder.
9. **Unchanged**: language model B4 with R1; price override tables A1; availability B1; tax cascade C1; units A1; recipes B4; ledger C1 and D; End Day as the founder defined it.

**Why**: each change is small, sourced at tier 2, and adds an attribute or a mode without changing a table's meaning. **Why not the others**: G2 duplicates the discount model; K3 contradicts the founder's kind list; T2 and T3 are coarser or more complex than the founder's per-branch rule; V1 alone leaves the made-then-voided case unanswered; C-a leaves a Tier 1 café without margin until every recipe is complete.
